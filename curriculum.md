<section class="section" style="padding-top:1rem">
  <div class="container">
    <div class="section-head reveal">
      <span class="eyebrow">The core</span>
      <h2>The reading, before you're onboarded</h2>
      <p>Read them the way a hacker reads a manual: to reason with, not to recite. The core is versioned; subjects get added as the field demands.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid grid-3">
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">01 · The constraint</span>
        <h3>A Theory of Justice</h3>
        <p>Every design here runs inside one hard boundary: justice for all. You cannot hold that boundary without a rigorous account of what fairness is. Rawls and the veil of ignorance, Sen on capabilities, and the strongest arguments against both. You should be able to state the case against your own position before you state your own.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">02 · How value moves</span>
        <h3>Modern Economics</h3>
        <p>Incentives, markets, public goods, externalities, game theory, mechanism design. Micro and behavioural. How people actually respond to payoffs, and exactly where the tidy models break. The physics of the field, in numbers.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">03 · Intent into outcome</span>
        <h3>Public Policy</h3>
        <p>The craft of turning a goal into a program that survives contact with reality: cost and benefit, piloting, measurement, rollback, second-order effects. Why good intentions produce exclusion errors. How to ship policy like code.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">04 · The field, formalised</span>
        <h3>Social Physics</h3>
        <p>How norms, information and behaviour propagate through networks of people, and how individual acts aggregate into the trajectory of a population. This is the gravity of our thesis, written as equations instead of metaphor.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">05 · The core discipline</span>
        <h3>Incentive Engineering</h3>
        <p>Aiming self-interest so the individually rational act is also the collectively good one. Mechanism design pointed at governance. This is the exhaust velocity of the whole project: making one honest act count for far more.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">06 · Seeing the machine</span>
        <h3>Systems Thinking</h3>
        <p>Feedback loops, stocks and flows, leverage points, why interventions backfire. Reading a whole system instead of its parts. The habit that separates a hacker from a commentator: you fix the structure, not the symptom.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="card reveal">
        <span class="kicker">07 · The substrate</span>
        <h3>Technology</h3>
        <p>Paper, printing and double-entry bookkeeping were once the technology of the state: they made evidence, records and dissemination possible. Today the substrate is cryptography, decentralised computing, peer-to-peer networks and AI. You study what each can remove: which choke point it deletes, which constraint it can enforce without a trusted middleman. And the critical half: where it only relocates the rent, and why centralised tech becomes its own gravity well. We favour what stays distributed.</p>
      </div>

<section class="thesis">
  <div class="container">
    <span class="eyebrow reveal">Why a bar</span>
    <p class="thesis-lead reveal">You cannot hack a system you have not read. <span class="muted">Opinions are cheap, and the country is drowning in them. Fluency is rare, and it is the whole difference between a member and a commentator.</span></p>
    <p class="reveal" style="color:var(--text-muted);font-size:1.08rem;max-width:720px">The bar is not there to keep people out. It is there so that when a member speaks, they are reasoning from the mechanism, not the mood. Anyone willing to do the reading can clear it. Most of politics is unwilling, and that is precisely the gap we are built to close.</p>
  </div>
</section>
